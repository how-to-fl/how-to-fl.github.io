Two of these have **legal deadlines**. The rest just make the following week possible. Work
down the list roughly in order — each step tends to need the one before it.

:::danger[The two with deadlines]
**Within 24 hours of arriving: register your address with the local police.**
**Within 30 days: convert your X1 visa into a residence permit.**

Both are legal requirements. The first is the single most forgotten thing on this site.
:::

## The chain, in order

Each of these lives in a different official PDF, and none of those PDFs mentions the others.
Laid end to end they're not eight separate errands — they're a chain, and two links produce
the piece of paper the next link demands.

## Day one

**☐ Get from the airport without being fleeced.** If you installed DiDi before you flew,
open it, type the address, and a metered car arrives at a price the app already told you —
that is the entire problem solved, and it is why it's on the
[pre-flight checklist](/china/before-you-fly/#the-checklist).

Otherwise walk to the **official taxi rank**: signposted, outside, with a queue. Three rules
cover the rest.

- **Never accept a ride from someone who approaches you inside the terminal.** That is the
  scam, and that is the whole of it — a legitimate Beijing taxi does not tout for business
  in arrivals. Arriving foreign students are the specific target.
- **Insist the meter goes on** (打表 — *dǎ biǎo*). If the driver won't, get out. This is
  normal and expected, not a confrontation.
- **Take the receipt** (发票 — *fā piào*) **and note the plate number.** It's how you get a
  bag back if you leave one behind, and it's why a driver behaves.

Roughly right: **¥100–130** from Capital (PEK), **¥180–230** from Daxing (PKX). Between
23:00 and 05:00 there's a legitimate night surcharge; a number in a different league from
those is not a surcharge. The [airport page](/china/airport-and-taxi-scams/) has the train
routes and the fuller version.

Show the driver this — **screenshot it before you fly**, because the moment you need it is
the moment you have no SIM and no data:

> <span lang="zh-CN">请带我去北京大学中关新园，地址是中关村北大街126号，谢谢！</span>
>
> Please take me to Zhongguanyuan Global Village, Peking University — No.126 Zhongguancun
> North Street, thank you.

**☐ Check in at Global Village reception**, in the front building. That's your room sorted.

**☐ Temporary residence registration.** Within 24 hours. If you're living in Global Village
the accommodation usually handles this for you as part of check-in — **but confirm that
they have, out loud, and get the slip.** Don't assume. If you're staying anywhere else, even
for a night, you do it yourself at the local police station with your passport and visa.

Keep the registration slip. You'll need it for the residence permit.

**☐ Get a Chinese SIM.** During arrival week the carriers set up **tents in Global Village**,
which is by far the easiest way to do it. Otherwise China Mobile at B1, dorm 45A. You need
a Chinese number before almost anything else will verify you.

**☐ Sleep.** Seriously. The jet lag from Europe is brutal and everything below is easier
after one proper night.

## Day two

**☐ Open a Chinese bank account** — see [banking](/china/banking/). Take your passport, your
admission letter and your address. It takes a while; go in the morning.

**☐ Set up Alipay and WeChat Pay** and link the bank account. Switch WeChat to the Chinese
version while you're at it — the menus stay English.

**☐ Activate your campus card** at B1-117, New Sun Student Center, then top it up through
Alipay or WeChat. This is your canteen money, library card and building access.

**☐ Register your face** for campus entry, if it didn't happen automatically — see
[campus life](/pku/campus-life/#getting-in-and-out-of-campus).

**☐ Get online.** Connect to `PKU` or `PKU secure`, then **log in at `its.pku.edu.cn`** —
connecting alone only reaches the internal network. September and October are prepaid.

## Day three, and the first fortnight

**☐ Start the residence permit.** 30-day deadline, and the FL team will send instructions —
read those emails. You'll need your passport, the police registration slip, the health
certificate and photos. Don't leave it to week four.

**☐ Book the medical examination**, unless you had it done at home. Most of us did it
here: about **¥30** at one of the specialised health-check centres, and the report is sent
straight to the Global Village front desk in Building 1 rather than handed back to you. The
residence permit needs it, so this is an early job, not a week-four one.

**☐ Buy the things for your room.** [Taobao](/misc/taobao/) delivers to the Global Village
mailroom, and it's cheaper than carrying it from home. Bedding, a kettle, an
[air purifier](/misc/seasonal-calendar/#air-quality), a drying rack.

**☐ Work out your route to Guanghua.** It's about ten minutes on foot. Do it once without
being late for something.

**☐ Go to the add/drop sessions.** The first two weeks are when you fix your timetable —
see [academics](/pku/academics/#registering-for-courses). Sit in on more than you'll keep.

**☐ Go on the PKU trips.** They run activities in the first fortnight — Great Wall,
Forbidden City. They're good, and more importantly that's where you meet everyone before
friendship groups set.

**☐ Put the [emergency numbers](/emergency/) in your phone.** Two minutes, and the one thing
here you hope stays useless.

## A small mercy

Everyone around you is also lost, jet-lagged and slightly overwhelmed — including the people
who look like they aren't. The first week is meant to feel like this. It gets normal
astonishingly fast.
