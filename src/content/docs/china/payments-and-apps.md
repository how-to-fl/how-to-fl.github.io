China runs on your phone to a degree that's hard to convey until you're standing in a shop
holding cash that nobody wants. Get these installed and working and the rest of the first
week goes far more smoothly.

## Install before you fly

While your app store, your bank's verification texts and the internet all still work
normally.

| App | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **WeChat** 微信 | Messaging, payments, mini-programs, your entire social life. Non-negotiable. |
| **Alipay** 支付宝 | Payments. The one most likely to accept a foreign card. |
| **Pleco** | The Chinese dictionary. Point the camera at a menu. Works offline. |
| **Papago** or Google Translate | Translation, including photo translation |
| **Amap** 高德 or **Baidu Maps** | Navigation. Amap integrates with Didi. |
| **Beijing Metro** 北京地铁 | Subway routing and ticketing |
| **Didi** 滴滴 | Taxis. Has an English version. |
| Air quality app | You'll check it more than the weather |

Also: your **VPN, on every device** — see [before you fly](/china/before-you-fly/#sort-your-vpn-before-you-fly),
because the sites you'd use to set one up are unreachable once you land.

## The order that actually works

This sequence matters — each step needs the one before it:

1. **Chinese SIM card.** Do this first; almost everything else wants a Chinese mobile
   number to verify against. Carriers set up **tents in Global Village** during arrival
   week, which is by far the easiest way — otherwise the China Mobile shop at B1, dorm 45A.
2. **Chinese bank account.** See [banking](/china/banking/).
3. **Link the bank account to WeChat** to switch on WeChat Pay.
4. **Switch WeChat to the Chinese version** — some features simply don't exist in the
   international one. The menus stay in English, so this costs you nothing.
5. **Campus card**, topped up via Alipay or WeChat — see
   [campus life](/pku/campus-life/#your-campus-card).

## Using a foreign card

Binding a foreign Visa or Mastercard to Alipay and WeChat Pay **does work now**, and it's a
huge improvement on a few years ago. It is not a complete substitute for a Chinese account:

- There are **transaction limits**, and small vendors sometimes fail on foreign cards
- Some services — a few ticketing platforms, some mini-programs — still refuse them
- Fees stack up on small everyday payments

So bind your foreign card on day one so you can eat, then open a local account when you
have time.

### When you still want cash

Rarely, but: some small canteens and street stalls, older taxi drivers, and the occasional
market. Keep a couple of hundred yuan in your room. Most of the year it'll sit there.

## WeChat — two warnings from people who learned the hard way

**Save phone numbers outside WeChat.** WeChat accounts get blocked, sometimes with no
explanation, and unblocking requires being verified by a Chinese friend who has a bank
account linked. If everything you have — every contact, every group, every coursework
thread — lives only inside WeChat, a block takes all of it at once. Keep a normal contact
list.

**And you cannot really function without it.** Coursework groups, club sign-ups, study room
bookings, campus card top-ups, your landlord, your professor — all WeChat. It isn't a social
app here, it's infrastructure.
