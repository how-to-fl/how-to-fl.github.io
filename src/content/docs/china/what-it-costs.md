Short version: **your rent and your health insurance are paid for, and you get a monthly
stipend on top.** What you spend beyond that is mostly flights and how much of China you
want to see.

These are real figures from the Mannheim cohort. Yours will differ — but the shape is right.

## The year, roughly

<ul class="fl-stats">
  <li><span class="n">€600–1300</span><span class="l">flights, there and back</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">€125</span><span class="l">X1 visa</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">€0</span><span class="l">accommodation — covered</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">€250–300</span><span class="l">food, per semester-ish</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">€300–500</span><span class="l">travel and going out</span></li>
</ul>

## What you get

- **A free room in Global Village.** A comparable room would otherwise be €300–500 a month,
  so this is the single biggest number on the page.
- **Health insurance** through the programme.
- **¥2,500 a month** stipend, during term (not the semester holidays).

Living on ¥2,500 alone is genuinely possible if you're careful and eat on campus. It is not
much if you want to be in Sanlitun every weekend. Most people top it up.

## What things cost

| | |
|---|---|
| Canteen meal on campus | **from about €2** (¥8–15) |
| Food delivery | €5–10 |
| A Western restaurant meal | €8–15 |
| Gym membership on campus | about **€20/month** |
| Campus canteen drink | around 20 cents |

Flights: **one-way from €300, return sometimes under €600.** Air China often has student
fares with two free checked bags, which matters more than it sounds when you're moving for
a year.

## Travelling

Trains are the good option, and cheaper than flying:

| | |
|---|---|
| High-speed train to Shanghai | ~€120 |
| Flight somewhere further, e.g. Chengdu | ~€100 |
| Hotel in Shanghai | from €30/night |
| Hotel in Chengdu | from €10/night |

Trips to neighbouring Asian countries can be surprisingly cheap; Japan and anywhere further
is not. See [getting out of Beijing](/lifestyle/travel/) — and remember your visa needs
converting to multiple-entry **before** you leave the country, which takes up to two weeks.

## Scholarships — apply for these

This is where the year stops being expensive.

**DAAD annual scholarship** (for German students) — over **€1,000 a month** for an academic
year, plus a flight allowance, plus an excellent international health insurance policy. It
**combines** with the FL scholarship and with the Begabtenförderungswerke. A lot of Mannheim
FL students have had it. With that combination you're studying more or less for free.

**CSC — Chinese Scholarship Council.** In practice almost every international student in
China receives this. It's straightforward money and there's no reason not to apply.

If you're not German, check what your own country's exchange funding bodies offer — the
equivalent almost always exists, and the FL team will know.

## Insurance, specifically

Two separate things, and you need to understand both:

- **PKU's insurance is compulsory: ¥800 per semester.** You don't get a choice.
- **It is not as good as a proper international policy.** In private or English-speaking
  hospitals — exactly the ones you'll want when something's actually wrong — costs climb
  fast and PKU's cover doesn't stretch far.

So take out international health insurance at home **as well**, before you fly. The DAAD
scholarship includes a good one. This is the cheapest regret to avoid on the whole list.
