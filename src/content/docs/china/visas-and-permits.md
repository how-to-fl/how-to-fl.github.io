Getting the residence permit is [day one to thirty](/china/first-72-hours/). This page is
everything after — the deadlines that come round again, and the ones you only meet when
something changes.

:::danger[The three clocks]
**30 days from landing** — convert the X1 into a residence permit.
**30 days *before* your permit expires** — apply to extend it. Not after.
**10 days from anything changing** — passport, name, address: report it.
:::

## Renewing your residence permit

Apply **within the 30 days before it expires**, at the exit–entry administration of the local
public security bureau. This is the one people read backwards: the window is the month
*before* expiry, not a grace period afterwards. If the permit lapses you are staying
illegally, and the fines are real.

Bring more time than you think. The authorities have **up to 15 days** to decide once you've
filed, and they keep your passport while they do — so a renewal and a trip abroad cannot
overlap.

The ISD runs an agent service on the third floor of the New Sun Student Center, which is the
path of least resistance and the one most of us take.

## If anything on the permit changes

If any registered detail changes — **your name, your passport, your reason for being here,
your address** — you have **10 days** to report it and have the permit altered. A new
passport is the common one: people renew at their embassy and don't realise the permit is now
attached to a document that no longer exists.

## Going multiple-entry before you travel

Your first residence permit may be single-entry. Leaving the country and expecting to come
back on it is how people get stranded.

**Convert it to multiple-entry before you book anything**, and budget up to two weeks. The
same passport-is-with-the-authorities problem applies, so this cannot be a last-minute job
before a trip.

## Re-registering with the police after every trip

The 24-hour registration rule isn't a one-off on arrival — it applies **every time you take
up residence at an address**, including coming back from a trip and including hotels within
China.

- **Hotels do it for you.** That's what the passport scan at check-in is.
- **Anywhere that isn't a hotel — a friend's flat, an Airbnb — is on you**, within 24 hours.
- **Coming home to Global Village**, check whether reception has re-registered you, and get
  the slip.

Keep the slips. The residence permit process asks for one, and so does anything else that
wants proof of address.

## Travelling inside China

Carry your **passport** — not a photocopy — when you travel domestically. It's needed for
train tickets, flights and hotel check-in, and you can be asked for it.

Some regions are closed to foreign visitors without prior authorisation. If you're planning
somewhere off the usual routes, check before you book rather than at the barrier.
