## Do this before you get on the plane

This is the single highest-value thing on the site, because it costs you ten minutes at home
and removes the entire problem.

1. **Install Alipay and connect a credit card** — at home, where the verification SMS reaches
   you and the app stores work.
2. **Install DiDi** (滴滴出行), China's Uber, and sign in.

Then when you land you open DiDi, type the address, and a metered car arrives at a price the
app already told you. No cash, no negotiation, no working out what a taxi should cost while
jet-lagged in a queue. See [banking](/china/banking/#the-plan-that-works) for the rest of the
money setup.

If you only manage one of the two, make it Alipay — most of the other apps will hang off it.

## The scam, and how not to fall for it

It's not elaborate. Someone approaches you **inside the terminal**, before you reach the rank,
and offers a taxi. That's the whole thing. A legitimate Beijing taxi does not tout for
business in arrivals.

What it costs you: a "fixed price" several times the meter, or a meter that has been got at.
Arriving foreign students are the specific target — unfamiliar with the prices, tired, and
carrying a year's luggage.

Three rules cover it:

- **Never accept a ride from someone who approaches you.** Walk to the official rank, which is
  signposted, outside, and has a queue.
- **Insist the meter goes on** (打表 — *dǎ biǎo*). If the driver won't, get out. This is normal
  and expected behaviour, not a confrontation.
- **Take the receipt** (发票 — *fā piào*) **and note the plate number.** It's how you get a bag
  back if you leave one behind, and it's why a driver behaves.

## Roughly what it should cost

So you can tell when a number is wrong:

| From | To campus | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Capital (PEK)**, 40 km NE | **¥100–130** by taxi, expressway toll included | 1–1.5 h |
| **Daxing (PKX)**, 54 km S | **¥180–230** by taxi | 1.5–2 h |

Between 23:00 and 05:00 there's a legitimate night surcharge. A number in a different league
from the ones above is not a surcharge.

## Or take the train

Cheaper, and immune to all of the above.

- **From Capital:** Airport Express (**¥25**) from Terminal 2 or 3 → change at Sanyuanqiao to
  Line 10 → change at Haidian Huangzhuang to Line 4 → **East Gate of Peking University**.
  Subway fare about ¥5. The Airport Express runs 06:35–23:10 from T2, 06:20–22:50 from T3.
- **From Daxing:** Daxing Airport Express (**¥35**) → change at Caoqiao to Line 19 → change at
  Mudanyuan to Line 10 → change at Haidian Huangzhuang to Line 4 → East Gate.

The catch is luggage: three changes with two suitcases at the end of a long-haul flight is a
lot. Most of us take the train if we're travelling light and a car if we're moving in.

## What to show the driver

You won't need to say anything. Show this:

> <span lang="zh-CN">请带我去北京大学中关新园，地址是中关村北大街126号，谢谢！</span>
>
> Please take me to Zhongguanyuan Global Village, Peking University — No.126 Zhongguancun
> North Street, thank you.

**Screenshot that before you fly**, along with [the arrival card](/misc/arrival-card/). The
moment you need it is the moment you have no SIM and no data.

## When you get there

Your first stop is the Global Village reception desk, which is staffed 24 hours — see
[your first 72 hours](/china/first-72-hours/). The clock on your
[24-hour police registration](/china/first-72-hours/) starts when you clear immigration, not
when you wake up.
