The things on this page are much harder to fix once you've landed. Work down it while
you're still at home.

## Visa

You'll be on an **X1** (study visa for stays over 180 days). Roughly **€125**.

What you need before you apply:

- **The JW202 form** and your admission letter — these come from PKU. Don't book flights
  until you have them
- **A medical examination**, done at home. The certificate must be **less than 6 months old
  when you arrive**, so don't do it too early either
- Passport valid well beyond the year — check the expiry now

Applications go through online forms, then you hand your passport in at the relevant office
and either collect it in person or get it posted back. Build in more time than the website
suggests.

:::note
An X1 gets you *into* China. Within 30 days of landing you convert it to a **residence
permit** — that's what actually lets you stay, and it's on your
[first 72 hours](/china/first-72-hours/) list.
:::

## Insurance

Two layers, and you want both:

- **PKU's insurance is compulsory — ¥800 per semester.** No choice, and it's fine for
  ordinary things.
- **A proper international policy from home**, because PKU's cover doesn't stretch far in
  private or English-speaking hospitals — precisely the ones you'll want if something
  serious happens.

If you're German and applying for **DAAD**, a very good international policy is included.
Details on [what the year costs](/china/what-it-costs/).

## Sort your VPN before you fly

This is the one that catches the most people, so it gets the most space.

**Why it matters for us specifically.** A lot of what your degree runs on is unreachable
from a mainland connection: Google — so Gmail, Drive, Scholar and Docs — plus most of the
apps you use to stay in touch with people at home. Your partner university almost certainly
runs on at least one of those.

**The catch.** The websites where you'd normally read about, buy, or download such a tool
are themselves generally unreachable from inside China. The window to set one up is
*before* you arrive. Every cohort has someone who didn't, and spent a fortnight borrowing a
friend's hotspot.

What we'd suggest:

- Set it up **on every device** — laptop, phone, tablet — and check each one works before
  you leave. A config that only lives on your laptop is no use in a queue with your phone
- **Have a second option installed.** Reliability varies week to week and a single point of
  failure is a poor plan for your email
- Know **how you'll pay from abroad**, and whether it renews on a card that'll still work
- **Download things while you can:** offline maps of Beijing and your arrival documents.
  Worth saving the pages of this guide you'll need on day one too — the site needs a
  connection, and yours may be patchy for the first few days

**On the legal side.** Personal use by foreign students is extremely common and openly
discussed on campus. It also sits in a genuine grey area under Chinese law, and the rules
are written loosely enough that we won't pretend otherwise. Two things are clear: don't sell
or share access, and don't make a public thing of it.

We're deliberately not naming providers or writing setup steps — this is a public page with
our names on it, and specifics age badly anyway. **Ask in the cohort chat**; someone who
arrived last year will tell you what's currently working, which beats anything we could pin
to a webpage.

**Also:** your university probably has its own VPN, and **PKU has one too**
(`vpn.pku.edu.cn`). Those are for reaching library databases and journals — a different job,
also worth installing. See [campus life](/pku/campus-life/#the-pku-vpn--not-the-one-youre-thinking-of).

## What you can't bring — medication rules matter here

**Read this properly.** Several medications that are ordinary at home are controlled or
outright illegal in China, and the consequences at the border are serious rather than
embarrassing.

- **ADHD stimulants** (methylphenidate, amphetamine-based medication) are the big one —
  treated as controlled narcotics
- Some strong painkillers and some sleep medication
- A few common cold and sinus remedies, because of what's in them

If you take anything prescribed, **check it specifically against Chinese customs rules
before you pack**, and talk to your doctor early about alternatives. Where something is
allowed, bring the **prescription and a doctor's letter**, keep it in the original labelled
packaging, and carry it in hand luggage.

This is not a rule to find out about at the airport.

## What to pack

**Bring:**

- A **serious winter coat** — Beijing hits −10 °C
- Summer clothes for 36 °C and humid. Both, because
  [spring and autumn barely happen](/misc/seasonal-calendar/)
- Any **prescription medication** (subject to the above), and anything your body
  specifically needs
- **Deodorant** if you're attached to a particular kind — it's oddly hard to find
- Shoes and clothes in your size if you're tall or take a large shoe size
- **Passport photos**. You'll be asked for them repeatedly
- Your **health certificate, JW202 and admission letter**, in print

**Don't bother:**

- Bedding, towels, kitchen things — buy them on [Taobao](/misc/taobao/) in week one, cheaper
- Everyday toiletries, paracetamol, plasters
- An air purifier — [get a Xiaomi one here](/misc/seasonal-calendar/#air-quality) and sell
  it to the next cohort when you go
- Large amounts of cash

## Learning some Chinese first

You do not need Chinese to survive — courses are in English and campus is navigable. But a
few weeks of **Pleco** and an app like **tandem** before you fly pays off immediately, and
the willingness to try is what gets you looked after. Numbers, food words, and "how much is
it" go a very long way.
