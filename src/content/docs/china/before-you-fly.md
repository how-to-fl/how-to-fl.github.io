## Sort your VPN before you fly

This is the one on the list that gets dramatically harder once you're through passport
control, so do it while you're still at home.

**Why it matters for us specifically.** A lot of what your degree runs on is unreachable
from a mainland connection: Google (so Gmail, Drive, Scholar and Docs), plus the usual
messaging and social apps you use to stay in touch with people at home. Your partner
university almost certainly runs on at least one of those. That's not a small
inconvenience — it's your email and your coursework.

**The catch.** The websites where you'd normally go to read about, buy, or download such a
tool are themselves generally unreachable from inside China. So the window to set one up is
*before* you arrive, not after. Every cohort has someone who didn't, and spent their first
fortnight borrowing a friend's hotspot.

**What we'd actually suggest:**

- Set it up **on every device** you're bringing — laptop, phone, tablet — and check each one
  actually works before you leave. A configuration that only lives on your laptop is no help
  when you're standing in a queue with your phone.
- Have a **second option** installed. Reliability varies a lot week to week, and a single
  point of failure is a bad plan for your email.
- Know how to **pay for it from abroad**, and whether your subscription renews on a card
  that'll still work.
- **Download things you'll need offline** while you're at it: offline maps of Beijing, your
  arrival documents, and this guide (it installs as an app and keeps working offline).

**On the legal side.** Personal use by foreign students is extremely common and openly
discussed on campus. It also sits in a genuine grey area under Chinese law, and the rules
are written loosely enough that we're not going to pretend to you that they're clear. Two
things that are clear: don't sell or share access, and don't make a public thing of it.

We're deliberately not naming providers or writing setup instructions here — this is a
public page with our names attached, and specifics age badly anyway. Ask in the cohort chat;
someone who arrived last year will tell you what's currently working, which is better
information than anything we could pin to a webpage.

**Also worth knowing:** PKU provides institutional access to a lot of academic resources
through the university network and library, so some of what you'd reach for a VPN for at
home is available directly on campus. Check that before assuming you need one for coursework.
