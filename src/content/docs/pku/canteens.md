There are more than ten canteens on campus and they are **absurdly cheap** — around ¥8–15
for a full meal, which is roughly €1–2. Most of us ate on campus most days, not to save
money but because it's genuinely easier than anything else.

## The ones worth knowing

| Canteen | | What it's for |
|---|---|---|
| **佟园** | Tong Yuan | **Halal, and widely reckoned the best food on campus.** Open to everyone after 17:00. |
| **农园** | Nong Yuan | Closest to the teaching buildings. Your default between classes. |
| **学五** | Xue Wu | Famous for the fried chicken leg with rice. Also has a coffee/quick-meal counter. |
| **学一** | Xue Yi | South of campus. |
| **家园** | Jia Yuan | The newest, widest variety. |
| **艺园** | Yi Yuan | **Night barbecue on the second floor.** |
| **燕南** | Yan Nan | Very crowded at noon — go early or late. |
| **勺园** | Shao Yuan | Southwest corner; has a Western food counter. |
| **松林** | Song Lin | Traditional baozi and porridge. Good breakfast. |
| **畅春园** | Chang Chun Yuan | Facing the west gate. |
| **小白房** | Xiao Bai Fang | Food stands, takeaway. |

Most take the campus card. **畅春园, 农园, 艺园, 勺园 (Western), 学五, 泊星地 and 最美时光
also take cash.**

**Go with someone who reads Chinese the first few times.** Menus are boards on the wall,
there's often no English, and pointing works better when someone can tell you what you're
pointing at. There's also a serve-yourself-then-pay canteen if you'd rather just look at
the food.

## Paying

Your **campus card** is the currency on campus — see [campus life](/pku/campus-life/) for
activating and topping it up. Top-ups happen through Alipay or WeChat, so you need one of
those working first.

Two things that trip people up:

- Above **¥30 in one meal** you'll be asked for your consumption password — the last six
  digits of your passport number. (If there's a letter in there, it's `888888`.)
- **Carry your passport.** There are ID checks on campus from time to time, and your card
  alone isn't always enough.

## If you can't eat everything

This matters and the canteens are not set up for it, so plan ahead. Get a card written in
Chinese explaining what you can't eat — allergy, vegetarian, vegan, halal, coeliac — and
show it rather than trying to explain.

Tong Yuan being halal-certified makes it the reliable option for a lot of people. For
everything else, printable cards are on our list to add here — if you've already made one
that works, **please send it to us**, it'll be the most-used thing on this site.

### Vegetarian, honestly

We'd rather tell you this now than let you find out in October.

**Eating vegetarian on campus is possible but genuinely harder than you're expecting.** There
is **less tofu in the canteens than most people assume**, and "vegetable" dishes are often
cooked with meat.

The two places that reliably work:

- **A vegan stall on the second floor of 家园 Jia Yuan**, the biggest canteen on campus — four
  storeys of it.
- **The 麻辣 málà stall on the third floor**, where you pick your ingredients and they cook
  them — so you can build something vegetarian with tofu.

Past those two, the options thin out quickly. That leaves cooking for yourself, and the
Global Village kitchens are sparse enough to make that a chore rather than a plan. The
realistic result is a **low-protein diet unless you're deliberate about it**, and more than
one of us arrived vegetarian and stopped being vegetarian here.

None of that is meant to talk you out of it. Plenty of people manage — it just takes more
planning than it does at home, and it's worth arriving with a strategy rather than an
assumption.
