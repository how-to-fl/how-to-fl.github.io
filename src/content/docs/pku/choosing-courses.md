Course selection is the first thing you do that has consequences a year later, and it's done
in a fortnight while you're jet-lagged. This is what we wish we'd known going in.

## Start with what *your* degree needs

Before you look at a single course description, get clear on this: **you are satisfying two
sets of graduation requirements at once**, and only you are looking at both.

Guanghua tells you what PKU needs. Your home university tells you what it needs. Nobody sits
in the middle holding the combined list — that job is yours, and the consequences of a gap
land on you two years later.

So work out, in writing, **which courses are compulsory for you** — for the PKU degree, and
for your home degree — before you start browsing. Then check it again each year, because
course offerings change and availability is not guaranteed. More on the mechanics in
[credit transfer](/pku/credit-transfer/).

**This matters more in spring than in autumn.** In your first semester the compulsory core
mostly fills your timetable for you. By spring you have real choice, which is exactly when
it becomes possible to build a semester you enjoy that quietly misses a requirement. Check
the mandatory list against your plan *before* add/drop closes, both times.

## PKUrCourses — built by our own cohort

People in Cohort 6 built a course evaluation and planning tool, and it's better than anything
official:

**[pkurcourses.lovable.app](https://pkurcourses.lovable.app/courses)**

It carries around 60 courses — mostly Guanghua, plus offerings from Life Sciences,
International Studies, Economics, the Law School, PE and Marxism — with, for each one:

- course code, title and credits
- **the instructor**
- the school it comes from
- language of instruction
- semester, autumn or spring
- **a student rating, and how many people rated it**

You can filter by semester, and log in to build your own schedule. The ratings are the part
you can't get anywhere else — the official system will tell you a course exists, not whether
it was any good.

:::note[Two caveats, neither fatal]
It's a **third-party site**, so unlike this guide it isn't served from our own origin and may
not load reliably from a mainland connection. And it depends on people keeping it going after
its authors graduate. Use it, and don't make it the only place your plan exists.
:::

## The three courses that don't behave normally

These caught people out in 2026 and they are not obvious from the course catalogue.

**中国管理（一）Chinese Management 1 · E2800020** shows a capacity of **zero** and you cannot
register for it. That's correct. The programme **adds it manually** for all Year 3 Future
Leaders students. What you must do is **check it doesn't clash with anything else you've
signed up for** — the system won't warn you about a course it doesn't think you're taking.

**世界政治经济环境中的中国 China in the Global Political Economy · E2800210** is **not in the
course selection system at all**. If you need it — it substitutes for Macroeconomics at
Guanghua — you email **futureleaders@gsm.pku.edu.cn** and ask. It starts later in the term
than everything else.

**商战模拟 Business Simulation Games · E2832231** finalises **enrolment and teams in the first
class**, and refuses late adds afterwards. If you want it, be in the room on day one.

## Other things worth knowing before you pick

- **商务中文 Business Chinese** — you sit a placement test, then register for level **B or C**
  according to your result. Register one, not both. If the test exempts you, register neither.
- **职业发展实践 Career Development Practice** — compulsory for Year 3 non-local students,
  optional for local ones. It runs as five or six sessions hosted at different companies
  rather than as normal classes, and the dates come separately.
- **中国营销 Marketing in China** — this is the one to take if you *didn't* do a marketing
  course at your home university.
- **沉浸式中国发展探索 China Immersion Project** — two preparation sessions in November and
  December, then a field trip in the last week of term. Budget the time.
- **金融工程 Financial Engineering** was previously called Risk Management. If you already took
  it under the old code, you can't take it again.

## Where you'll actually be

Most Future Leaders teaching happens in **Guanghua Building 1** — rooms 111, 113, 114, 202,
215, 216 and 218, plus the computer room for the simulation course. Some electives sit in the
**2nd Teaching Building**, the **1st Teaching Building**, or the School of International
Studies. All of them are on [the campus map](/pku/campus-map/).

Class periods run from 8am to half nine at night — the numbered slots are on
[academics](/pku/academics/#class-times).

## Looking beyond Guanghua

There are English-taught courses across the university that count as general education
electives, and they're a genuine opportunity rather than a box to tick — Chinese courts,
non-traditional security, Chinese perspectives on international affairs, taijiquan philosophy
and practice. Not every English-taught course at PKU is listed in the FL materials; the
selection system has more if you go looking.
