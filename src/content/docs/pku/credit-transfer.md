## How to read this page

Credit transfer has two halves, and they behave completely differently.

**The PKU half is the same for all of us** and it's written down — that's the first section
below, and it's the part we can tell you confidently.

**The partner-school half is different at every school**, isn't written down anywhere central,
and is the reason this page exists. Those sections are still empty. If you've been through it
at your school, filling in even a few lines is a proper contribution.

## The PKU side, which is the same for everyone

You are doing **one degree of 120 units**, of which **at least 60 must be taken at Guanghua**.
Years 1 and 2 happen at your home university, years 3 and 4 here.

**The recognition is already agreed; the paperwork is not.** Guanghua and your school have
settled which courses count for which — but you still have to **run each transfer through
PKU's online system yourself**. Nobody does it for you, and the agreement existing is not the
same as the credit appearing.

**Confirm your plan with both institutions every year.** Course offerings change and
availability isn't guaranteed. You are the only person looking at both sets of graduation
requirements at once, and the consequences of a gap land on you.

### What transferred credits do to your transcript

This surprises people, so it's worth being precise:

- Transferred courses **appear on your PKU transcript with the grades your home school gave
  them**.
- Those grades **do not count towards your PKU GPA**. Your PKU GPA is built only from what you
  take here.
- PKU transcripts show **numerical grades only** — no letters — with a note explaining the
  conversion.

`EX` exempt, `IP` in progress, `I` incomplete, `NP` not pass, `P` pass and `W` withdrawal all
sit outside the GPA calculation.

### The three thresholds that actually bite

:::danger
**GPA below 2.0** puts you on academic probation, and you'll be asked to consider whether to
continue at Guanghua or go back to your home programme — which means giving up the Guanghua
degree.

**Fail 20 units or more and you cannot receive a PKU degree at all**, even if you go back and
pass everything afterwards. This one has no remedy.

**Fail two courses and the rest of your stipend stops.** See
[what the year costs](/china/what-it-costs/).
:::

A softer one worth knowing: if you fail a **core** course but stay above 2.0, you have to
**retake it**. Sitting the exam again isn't enough — PKU wants the course.

And **attendance is compulsory** in Guanghua courses, counting towards the final grade in most
of them. It isn't a formality.

### Finishing, and not finishing on time

You're expected to complete Guanghua's requirements **by the end of your second year here**.
If you can't, you can apply for a **one-year extension — at your own cost**, which means
tuition, accommodation and everything else that was previously covered.

The two degrees are issued **independently**, and can arrive at different times. Finishing one
institution's requirements and not the other's means you get that degree and still owe the
other one its remaining work.

## Getting your PKU credits back home

Ask your home school early — the timeline and rules for taking PKU credits *back* are theirs,
not PKU's, and they are not symmetrical with the rules for bringing credits here. Nobody has
written up how this went for them yet.

## Who to ask when it's unclear

The **Programme Coordinator** is the first point of contact, and the FL team address is
**futureleaders@gsm.pku.edu.cn**. Course selection specifically gets covered properly during
orientation week, which is the moment to ask the awkward questions rather than the polite
ones.

For anything that touches your home degree, ask your home school **as well** — the FL team
can tell you what PKU requires and genuinely cannot tell you what Mannheim or Bocconi will
accept.
