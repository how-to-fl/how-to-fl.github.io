PKU is a serious university and the workload is real — but it's distributed differently from
what most of us are used to. Less "one terrifying exam", more "something due every week".

## The year

<ul class="fl-stats">
  <li><span class="n">18</span><span class="l">weeks, autumn semester (Sept–Jan)</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">18</span><span class="l">weeks, spring semester (late Feb–late June)</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">8–10</span><span class="l">weeks summer break, from late June</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">4–6</span><span class="l">weeks winter break, set by Chinese New Year</span></li>
</ul>

**There are no days off for Christmas, Easter or Thanksgiving.** The university follows the
Chinese public holiday calendar, so if you're used to a December break, plan for it not
existing. The long ones are summer, and the winter break around Chinese New Year — which
moves every year with the lunar calendar.

Within the semester, [Golden Week](/misc/seasonal-calendar/) in early October and the May
break are the two big pauses.

## Class times

Teaching runs from 8am to half nine at night. Which slots you actually get depends entirely
on your course choices.

| | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** 08:00–08:50 | **II** 09:00–09:50 | **III** 10:00–11:00 | **IV** 11:10–12:00 |
| **V** 13:00–13:50 | **VI** 14:00–14:50 | **VII** 15:10–16:00 | **VIII** 16:10–17:00 |
| **IX** 17:10–18:00 | **X** 18:40–19:30 | **XI** 19:40–20:30 | **XII** 20:40–21:30 |

Courses are usually two or three of these blocks together. Class sizes run about 30–50.

## Registering for courses

Registration is online, and it opens after the previous semester's exams — so as a new
arrival you select once you've completed registration.

**The first two weeks of term are the add/drop window.** This is when you fix your
timetable, and you should treat it as a real part of the semester rather than an
afterthought: sit in on more than you'll keep, then drop.

Two rules that catch people:

- **You can't select courses that clash** unless you're in a graduating class. The system
  simply won't let you.
- **14–25 credits per semester** is the allowed range.

There's also a mid-term withdrawal, normally around week 8. The course then shows on your
transcript as **W**.

## How grading actually works

Grades are percentages (or Pass/Fail). **60% is a pass.** GPA is out of 4.00, where 100%
is a 4.00.

A typical course breaks down something like:

<ul class="fl-stats">
  <li><span class="n">50%</span><span class="l">final exam or term paper</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">20%</span><span class="l">participation</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">20%</span><span class="l">presentation</span></li>
  <li><span class="n">10%</span><span class="l">weekly assignments</span></li>
</ul>

That's the real difference from home: **participation and presentations are graded**, and
the work is spread through the semester rather than piled onto an exam period. Most of us
found the exam weeks less brutal than at our home university, and the middle of term
busier.

### What the numbers mean in practice

If you're converting to a German grade: **98+ is very rarely awarded** in the Chinese
system, so a 1.0 is not really the target. A **1.3–1.7 is realistic** with sensible course
choices, which means sitting somewhere around 85–95 in most subjects.

### The rules with teeth

These are the ones worth knowing before you drift:

- **Cumulative GPA below 2.0 → no bachelor's degree from PKU.**
- **Below 1.5 for two consecutive semesters, plus more than 12 failed units → expulsion.**

Neither is a risk if you're turning up and doing the work, but they aren't decorative
either.

### Attendance is compulsory and recorded

Attendance is taken for lectures, tests and exams. If you need to miss, be late, or leave
early, you submit a written request to the professor **in advance**.

**Signing in for someone else is explicitly prohibited** and treated as an integrity
offence. Don't — it's not worth it for anyone involved.

## Exams

Final exam week is in the academic calendar and runs two weeks. Your slot shows on the
"course selection results" page; if it's blank, ask the professor.

If you're ill or something happens before an exam, it can be postponed — but it appears on
your transcript as **I** (Incomplete), and **you must apply in advance**. Missing an exam
without prior permission is a zero.

## Finding your grades

**Your GPA:** the PKU portal, "My GPA" module.

**Your ranking:** Guanghua's system at `crm.gsm.pku.edu.cn` — first login is your student ID
as the username, and your birth date as `yyyymmdd` (or `00000000`) as the password. Then
常用功能 → 成绩查询.

Handy characters for that page: 学年度 school year · 学期 semester · 学号 student ID ·
年级排名 year ranking · 综合GPA overall GPA · 核心课GPA core-course GPA

## Transcripts

**They are not sent automatically — you have to ask.** Three ways:

1. **The self-service machines** at the library, New Sun Student Center, and some
   department buildings. Chinese-only interface: 校内门户认证 → 公文制作 → 成绩单.
2. **In person** at the Office of Educational Administration, New Sun Student Center B106
   (010-62751406). Bring your campus card.
3. **Online** via `dean.pku.edu.cn`.

Available in English or Chinese, paper or electronic. If your home university needs one for
credit transfer, order it earlier than you think you need to.

## Your professors

Mostly Chinese faculty with excellent English, very often with doctorates from Wharton,
Princeton, Yale or Oxbridge, and many of them advise government or sit close to Chinese
industry. The guest lectures and company visits are genuinely one of the best parts of the
year — go to them.

And your classmates are not messing about: a lot of the locals in Guanghua were top-five in
their province in the gaokao, out of a population the size of a large European country.
It's a humbling and quite motivating room to be in.
