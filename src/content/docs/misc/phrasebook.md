Everything here is meant to be **shown, not spoken**. Nobody expects your tones to work in
week one, and holding up a phone with the right characters on it solves the problem just as
well.

Screenshot the two address cards below **before you fly**. The one moment you'll want them
most — standing at the airport taxi rank with no data and no SIM — is the one moment you
can't load this page.

## Telling someone what you can't eat

<span lang="zh-CN">素食</span> and <span lang="zh-CN">不要辣</span> above will get you a long
way, and they are as far as the official booklet goes.

**Allergies are the gap, and it's the one that matters most.** Nobody has written up how to
say "I'm allergic to peanuts" in a way a canteen worker will act on, and that is a genuinely
unsafe thing to be missing. If you have an allergy and you've worked out how to communicate
it here — including whether anyone took it seriously — please add it.

## Emergencies

Dial these directly. They're free from any phone, including one with no SIM.

<ul class="fl-emergency">
	<li><span class="n">110</span><span class="l">Police</span></li>
	<li><span class="n">120</span><span class="l">Ambulance</span></li>
	<li><span class="n">119</span><span class="l">Fire</span></li>
	<li><span class="n">122</span><span class="l">Traffic accident</span></li>
</ul>

**110 also takes text messages on 12110**, which is worth knowing if you can't safely speak
or your Chinese deserts you.

Save your own embassy's 24-hour emergency line as a contact before you fly — every embassy
has one, it's on their website, and it is not something you want to be searching for at the
time. The rest of the numbers you might need are on
[when something's gone wrong](/emergency/).
