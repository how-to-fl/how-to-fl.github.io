## What's legal on an X1 visa

**Start here, before you start applying.** A study residence permit is permission to study,
not permission to work. Taking an internship — paid or unpaid — can require **separate
clearance**, and doing it without is a problem with your immigration status rather than an
administrative slip.

The programme is explicit that understanding the conditions of your permit is **your**
responsibility, and equally explicit that they'll help: **Guanghua will guide you through
applying for additional clearance** if you want to do an internship. So the sequence is ask
first, apply second — not the other way round.

Ask the FL team at **futureleaders@gsm.pku.edu.cn** before you accept anything.

## The one built for us

Guanghua's **Career Development Center (CDC)** has a career module **specifically for the
Future Leaders programme** — career planning, understanding the Chinese business market,
interview skills and networking. It exists because our situation isn't the same as a
domestic student's, and most people don't realise it's there.

There's also a credit-bearing version: **职业发展实践 Career Development Practice
(E2800340)**, which runs as five or six sessions hosted *inside different companies* rather
than as normal classes. It's **compulsory for Year 3 non-local students** and optional for
local ones — so for most of us it isn't a choice, and it's a better deal than it sounds.
See [choosing courses](/pku/choosing-courses/).

## Finding an internship in Beijing

Three places that post them:

- **The International Students Division** keeps a running list of opportunities for
  international students specifically — the ones where your visa status has already been
  thought about.
- **PKU's Student Career Center** — `scc.pku.edu.cn` — is the university-wide service.
- **The CDC**, for anything Guanghua-specific.

Beyond the listings, the honest answer is that most internships here come through people:
your class WeChat groups, the alumni your professors know, and company visits from the
course above. Which makes the guest lectures and company visits worth treating as more than
a free afternoon.
