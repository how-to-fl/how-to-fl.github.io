This is a guide to arriving in Beijing on the Future Leaders program, written by the
people who did it a year before you. It is not official, it is not produced by the
program office, and it is only as good as what the last cohort bothered to write down.

## Which bits to trust

Every page carries the month someone last confirmed it was true.

- **"Last checked August 2026"** under the title — someone verified it then.
- **An orange banner** — nobody has checked in over a year. Prices, opening hours and
  especially visa rules move. Treat it as a starting point, not an answer.
- **"This page is a stub"** — the structure exists, the content doesn't. Yet.

Nothing here overrides what the program office or the PSB tells you. Where this guide
disagrees with an official source, the official source wins and **the guide is wrong** —
please report it.

## Where to start

If you are arriving soon, in this order:

1. [Before you fly](/arrival/before-you-fly/) — while you can still change what's in your suitcase.
2. [Your first 72 hours](/arrival/first-72-hours/) — the checklist, including the police
   registration that is a legal requirement and the single most forgotten thing on this site.
3. [Getting in from the airport](/arrival/arriving-at-the-airport/) — read on the plane.

Everything else can wait until you are here.

## How to add what you know

You do not need to know how to code.

**If GitHub loads for you:** click the pencil icon at the bottom of any page, or
[open an issue form](https://github.com/how-to-fl/how-to-fl.github.io/issues/new/choose)
to add a place to the map.

**If GitHub does not load** — and from a Beijing connection it often won't — send it by
WeChat or email to a maintainer, or bring it to the next contribution sprint. Someone
will type it in. Your name still goes on it.

The information in your head is the scarce thing. The tooling is not, and it should
never be the reason something goes unwritten.

## The one rule for map pins

Coordinates must be **WGS-84**. Amap (高德) and Baidu use deliberately offset systems,
and a coordinate copied from either lands 300–500 metres from where you meant.

Find the place on [openstreetmap.org](https://www.openstreetmap.org), right-click it,
choose "Show address" — the numbers in the URL are the right ones. If in doubt, leave
the coordinates blank and describe the location instead.

## Who runs this

The **Guide Steward** role passes from each cohort to the next every May, along with a
sweep in which every section owner re-checks their pages. If you want to own a section,
say so — it is one page, once a year, and it is what keeps this alive after the person
who started it has graduated.
