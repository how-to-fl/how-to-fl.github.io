## Your two layers of insurance

Worth understanding *before* you need to use them:

1. **PKU's insurance is compulsory — ¥800 per year**, bought before you enter the mainland.
   Everyone has it, and Guanghua pays the basic premium back with your stipend.
2. **It covers general departments of public hospitals in the mainland, and that's the
   limit.** In private and English-speaking hospitals — the ones you'll actually want when
   something's wrong — costs escalate quickly and PKU's cover doesn't go far.

Two practical things about claiming on the PKU policy: **ask the doctor to print your medical
records** while you're there, because you'll need them, and if you're admitted the scheme can
pay the hospital directly — but somebody has to ring **4008105119** to start that. They have
an English line. The stamp you need for reimbursement comes from the ISD in the New Sun
Student Center.

So take out a proper international health policy at home as well. If you're on a **DAAD
scholarship, a good one is included**. See [what the year costs](/china/what-it-costs/#insurance-specifically).

Find out what your policy needs from you **before** you're ill: some want you to call them
first, most want the original itemised receipts, and Chinese hospitals will give you those
if you ask at the time — and not afterwards.

## Where to go

**Something minor** — the **PKU Campus Hospital**, on campus. Bring your campus card and
make an appointment. Cheap, close, and fine for a chest infection or a sprain.

**Something you need to discuss properly, in English** — **United Family, Wudaokou** is the
one most of us end up at. 1F Building D, Tsinghua Tongfang Hi-Tech Plaza, 1 Wangzhuang Lu,
Haidian. 010-8236 6918, emergency **010-5927 7120**. It's private and it is expensive —
which is exactly what the international insurance is for.

Two reasons it keeps winning despite the price: **everybody there speaks English**, and the
booking system works **online and by phone around the clock**, with specialist appointments
available at genuinely short notice. Compared with turning up at a public hospital and
queueing, that difference is worth a lot on a bad day. United Family has several sites around
Beijing — check which one you're booking before you set off.

**Peking Union Medical College Hospital** runs an International Medical Services department
(010-6915 6114) and is one of the best hospitals in the country.

**PKU Third Hospital** (49 Huayuan North Rd, Haidian — 010-8226 6699) is the big general
hospital nearest campus.

The full list is on the [emergency page](/emergency/).

### How a Chinese hospital works

It's not like home and the first visit is disorienting. Broadly: you register at a desk
first (挂号), pay before treatment rather than after, and carry the paperwork between
departments yourself. Take your passport, your campus card, and if you possibly can, **take
a friend who speaks Chinese** — for anything beyond the campus clinic, it makes an enormous
difference.

## Mental health

The year is brilliant and it is also hard. New language, no daylight in January, family
twelve time zones away, and everyone around you looking like they've got it sorted. Feeling
flattened at some point is *completely normal* and not a sign you're doing it wrong.

**PKU Psychological Health and Counseling Center**

- 4th floor, New Sun Student Center
- **English consultations are available** — say so when you book
- Book online at `sps.pku.edu.cn`, or 010-62760852
- **24-hour hotline: 010-62760521**
- Bring your campus card

The centre explicitly covers things people talk themselves out of asking about: trouble
adapting, low mood, friction with classmates or teachers, procrastination that's got out of
hand. You don't need a crisis to book.

Talk to each other, too. Someone in your cohort is having the same week.

## Sexual health and contraception

Condoms are sold in every convenience store and supermarket, no fuss. The contraceptive pill
is available but brands differ from home — **if you take a specific one, bring enough for
the year**, and check its legal status first (see
[before you fly](/china/before-you-fly/#what-you-cant-bring--medication-rules-matter-here)).
International clinics like United Family handle sexual health and STI testing discreetly and
in English.

## Pharmacies and everyday things

Pharmacies (药店) are everywhere and pharmacists will help you if you show them the problem
or a translated note. Pleco's camera is very good for reading a box.

Bring from home: anything prescription, plus whatever your body specifically needs. Don't
bother bringing paracetamol or plasters — they're here and cheaper.
