Everywhere we've recommended. Click a pin for the details, and hit **Copy address** to get
the Chinese address onto your clipboard — that's the bit you show a taxi driver or paste
into Didi.

<BeijingMap />

**The whole subway is drawn underneath everything** — all 26 lines in their own colours and
every one of the 403 stops — so you can see at a glance whether somewhere is a straight shot
down one line or an awkward three-changes-and-a-walk. It stays visible however far you zoom
out; line names appear from a middling zoom and station names as you get closer. Toggle it
off with the **Subway** button if it's in your way.

**Moving around:** use the **+ / −** buttons, or hold <kbd>ctrl</kbd> (<kbd>⌘</kbd> on a Mac)
while you scroll. We made scrolling need a modifier key on purpose, so the map doesn't
hijack the page when you're just reading past it. The **⛶** button goes fullscreen, which is
what you want if you're actually planning an evening, and the **◎** button finds you if
you're already out and lost. Tap a category and we'll zoom you to those pins.

## Adding somewhere

Two ways, neither of which involves writing code.

**If GitHub loads for you**, [fill in the "add a place" form](https://github.com/how-to-fl/how-to-fl.github.io/issues/new/choose)
— five fields, and one of us merges it.

**If GitHub won't load** — and from Beijing it often won't — WeChat or email it to one of
us, or bring it to the next contribution session. Someone will add it for you.

### Getting the coordinates right

This is the one bit worth being fussy about.

China mandates an offset coordinate system. **Amap (高德) and Tencent use GCJ-02, Baidu uses
BD-09**, and our map uses WGS-84 — the same thing GPS and OpenStreetMap use. Anything you
copy out of a Chinese map app lands **300–500 metres** from where you meant, which is far
enough to send someone to the wrong side of a six-lane road.

To get one that works: find the place on
[openstreetmap.org](https://www.openstreetmap.org), right-click the spot, choose "Show
address", and read the numbers out of the URL.

Honestly though — if you're not sure, **just leave the coordinates out** and tell us roughly
where it is. A blank is a two-minute job for whoever merges it; a wrong one is invisible
until somebody's lost. Our build rejects anything outside greater Beijing, so a wildly wrong
number gets caught, but one that's merely 400 m off sails straight through.

## Why the map looks like this

The basemap is a single 40 MB file we serve ourselves, not something borrowed from Google or
Amap. Your browser only pulls the few kilobytes it needs for wherever you're looking.

That's deliberate. A map that leans on someone else's service is a map that dies the day
that service becomes unreachable from here — and it'd take the rest of the page down with
it. Chinese labels get drawn using the fonts already on your device.
