## Adding a place

Two ways, and neither needs you to write code.

**If GitHub loads for you**, [open the "add a place" form](https://github.com/how-to-fl/how-to-fl.github.io/issues/new/choose)
— five fields, and a maintainer merges it.

**If GitHub does not load** — and from a Beijing connection it often won't — send it by
WeChat or email, or bring it to the next contribution sprint. Someone will add it.

### Getting the coordinates right

This is the one thing worth being careful about.

China mandates an offset coordinate system. **Amap (高德) and Tencent use GCJ-02, Baidu
uses BD-09**, and this map uses WGS-84, the same system as GPS and OpenStreetMap. A
coordinate copied out of a Chinese map app lands **300–500 metres** from where you meant
— far enough to point at the wrong side of a six-lane road.

To get a correct coordinate: find the place on
[openstreetmap.org](https://www.openstreetmap.org), right-click the spot, and choose
"Show address". The numbers in the URL are WGS-84.

If you are not sure, **leave the coordinates out** and describe where it is instead.
A missing coordinate is a small job for a maintainer; a wrong one is invisible until
somebody is lost. The build also rejects anything outside greater Beijing, so a badly
mistaken coordinate gets caught automatically — but one that is merely 400 m off will not be.

## Why the map looks like this

The basemap is a single 40 MB file served from this site, not from Google or Amap. Your
browser reads only the few kilobytes it needs for the area you are looking at.

That is deliberate: a map that depends on a third-party service is a map that stops
working the day that service becomes unreachable, and it would take the rest of the page
down with it. Chinese labels are drawn using your own device's fonts.
