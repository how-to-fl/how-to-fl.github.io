**999** also reaches an ambulance — it's a separate service that some people find answers
faster. Either is fine.

If you can, have a Chinese-speaking friend make the call, or hand your phone to someone
nearby. Operators may not speak English.

with hospitals, with your home university. That's genuinely what they're there for.

## If you lose your passport

1. Report it to the police and get the report — you need the paperwork.
2. Tell your embassy; they issue the replacement travel document.
3. Tell the International Students Division and the FL team, because your residence permit
   is tied to the passport and has to be reissued too.

Day to day, **don't carry your passport around**. Your campus card plus a photocopy or
photo of the passport is enough to identify you almost everywhere. Keep the real one in
your room.

## Scams and fraud

Be blunt about this one: **nobody legitimate will ever ring you and ask you to transfer
money.** Not the police, not immigration, not your bank, not "the embassy". The scam that
targets international students in China is a phone call claiming there's a problem with your
visa or a parcel, and it is convincing enough that it catches people every year.

Hang up. Then call the FL team or the police on 110 yourself, on a number you looked up.

More on the everyday cons in [scams and staying safe](/china/scams-and-safety/).
